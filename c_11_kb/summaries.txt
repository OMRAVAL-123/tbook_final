Chapter 1: Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry
This chapter introduces the foundational principles of chemistry including 
the nature and classification of matter, measurement systems, significant figures, 
laws of chemical combinations, Dalton’s atomic theory, atomic and molecular masses, 
mole concept, percentage composition, empirical and molecular formulas, and 
stoichiometric calculations. It builds the numerical and conceptual base required 
for all further chemical studies.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 2: Structure of Atom
This chapter explains the discovery of subatomic particles and the evolution 
of atomic models from Thomson and Rutherford to Bohr and the quantum mechanical model. 
It covers electromagnetic radiation, Planck’s theory, atomic spectra, dual nature of matter, 
Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, quantum numbers, atomic orbitals, electronic configuration, 
and stability of half-filled and fully filled subshells.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 3: Classification of Elements and Periodicity
This chapter discusses the need for classification of elements and the development 
of the periodic table. It explains the modern periodic law, electronic configurations, 
block elements (s, p, d, f), metals and non-metals, and periodic trends such as atomic radius, 
ionization enthalpy, electron affinity, electronegativity, and chemical reactivity.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure
This chapter explores ionic and covalent bonding, the octet rule, Lewis structures, 
bond parameters, VSEPR theory, valence bond theory, hybridisation, and molecular orbital theory. 
It explains bond formation, bond strength, resonance, polarity, hydrogen bonding, and bonding 
in homonuclear diatomic molecules, forming the structural basis of chemistry.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 5: Thermodynamics
This chapter introduces thermodynamic concepts including system and surroundings, 
internal energy, work, enthalpy, calorimetry, enthalpy changes of reactions, spontaneity, 
entropy, and Gibbs free energy. It establishes the energy relationships governing chemical 
and physical processes.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 6: Equilibrium
This chapter covers equilibrium in physical and chemical processes, dynamic equilibrium, 
law of mass action, equilibrium constant, Le Chatelier’s principle, ionic equilibrium, acids 
and bases (Arrhenius, Brønsted-Lowry, Lewis concepts), pH scale, buffer solutions, hydrolysis, 
solubility product, and common ion effect.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 7: Redox Reactions
This chapter explains oxidation and reduction in terms of classical concepts and electron transfer. 
It discusses oxidation numbers, balancing redox reactions, redox titrations, electrode processes, 
and the role of redox reactions in chemical and electrochemical systems.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 8: Organic Chemistry – Some Basic Principles and Techniques
This chapter introduces fundamental concepts of organic chemistry including structural 
representation, IUPAC nomenclature, isomerism, reaction mechanisms, electron displacement effects, 
resonance, inductive effect, hyperconjugation, purification techniques, and qualitative and 
quantitative analysis of organic compounds.
----------------------------------------
Chapter 9: Hydrocarbons
This chapter discusses classification and properties of hydrocarbons including alkanes, 
alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic compounds. It explains nomenclature, isomerism, preparation methods, 
structural features of benzene, aromaticity, directive influence in substitution reactions, and 
the environmental and health impacts such as carcinogenicity and toxicity.
----------------------------------------
